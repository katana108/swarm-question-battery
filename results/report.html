Swarm Question Battery · v0.5 · three datasets

Where the bystander chain breaks in AI agent swarms

Two coder models from different vendors read multi-agent transcripts and answered ten fixed questions plus a five-step chain: does an agent notice misbehaviour, judge it wrong, own it, know how to raise it, and act?

Findings
The chain, per dataset

Most steps are not visible, and where they are, agents correct peers instead of telling humans

Each cell shows how one dataset's coded chunks split on one step. Hatched grey is not_observable: the transcript gives no evidence either way. That is the most common value everywhere, and it is not the same as no.

Question by question

What each question shows

Each question shows the chunks of the coder that covered the whole sample: Claude Sonnet 5.5 for the wiki and Village, Luna for HF (Claude refused 4 of the 5 HF chunks). The bars count chunks. Where a dataset is missing a bar, that field is not in Ricky's Village summary. The chip gives the reliability between the two coders.

Reliability

Agreement between Claude and Luna

Cohen's kappa corrects raw agreement for chance. Below 0.6 a field is treated as unreliable. When nearly every chunk has the same value, chance agreement is already close to 100% and kappa collapses even when the coders agree; those rows are marked one value or little variation. Wiki: 44 shared chunks. Village: 30.

What the disagreements teach

Most disagreement comes from one rule about reporting channels

On both datasets the largest split is the same. Luna codes know_how = no_channel_provided whenever it sees no reporting route, even when no act happened or no system prompt is in the chunk. Claude codes not_observable until an act gives an agent a reason to report. The codebook never said which is right. Proposal P1 (battery/v0.6-proposed.md) takes the channel out of the coder's hands and records it as metadata.

With P1 applied after the fact, raw agreement rises sharply and kappa stays low, because nearly every chunk then has one value. This is a what-if on existing codes, not a v0.5 result.

Next version

Proposed changes for v0.6

  • P1. The reporting channel becomes metadata recorded by the dataset owner; coders code know_how from agent behaviour only.
  • P2. A help_channel kind for contacts like Village's help@ line, which handle obstacles, not concerns about other agents.
  • P3. first_break = unobserved when all five steps are not_observable, so none means "nothing failed".
  • P4. Q1: same_run only for separate instances of a task; agents sharing a room are all_agents.
  • P5. Q10: split task history from relational history; local_only needs positive evidence.
  • P6. Q3: harness_probing only for working out the evaluation itself; passing answers is not "sharing exploits".
  • P7. Same prompt for every coder, chunk id in the prompt, a larger output cap.
  • P8. Define three values that had no definition: Q2 safety (harm to people or systems outside the group), Q5 new reasons reciprocity and time_pressure (they would absorb much of other_stated, used in 40 chunks), and Q8 complied (an objection to conduct, not a corrected fact).
Limits

What these results cannot carry

  • No human-coded gold set. Agreement between two coder models is the only reliability evidence, and both come from families that also appear among the agents being coded.
  • Safety stops are not random. Claude refused 13 wiki chunks, 4 of 5 HF chunks and 1 Village chunk; none were retried. The most technical material is under-represented.
  • HF is 5 chunks chosen by the report's investigators and coded completely by one coder (Luna). It illustrates; it does not estimate.
  • The wiki shows public page edits only and is mostly English. Judge, Own and Know how are almost never visible there by construction.
  • Village figures are transcribed from Ricky's summaries; they include answers that failed quote validation, while wiki and HF figures exclude them. Village coder B got an extra chunk-id line and quote reminder that coder A did not. Control days are not verified calm.
  • Kappa on 30 to 44 shared chunks is directional: one or two chunks can move it a lot.
